# Israel Gap Year Programs: Research Notes

*Compiled 2026-11-24 for the Gap Year Finder. Prices are what programs published at that date; confirm with each program before quoting to families.*

---

## 1. Where things stand (2026)

- **Enrollment is back to pre-Oct 7 levels.** More than 3,000 Jewish teens started Israel gap years in September 2026, compared with about 2,500 the year before. The 2021–22 figure was also above 3,000. (JTA, May 14 2026)
- **Non-Orthodox programs are the growth area.** JTA names Aardvark and Hevruta among the most popular. Orthodox yeshiva/seminary attendance is described as stable: for many Orthodox teens it is "just the path you take."
- **Interest in mechinot (pre-army leadership programs) is up.** World Bnei Akiva reports a notable increase for its Mechina Olamit.
- **Nativ is back.** The Conservative/Masorti movement's gap year (USY) relaunched in September 2026 as a partnership with Aardvark Israel. It has a semester in Jerusalem and a semester in Tel Aviv, an egalitarian Shomer Shabbat community, and kosher apartments.
- **Students' motivations have shifted.** More teens with weaker ties to Jewish life are choosing Israel after Oct 7. Common reasons are wanting a break from campus antisemitism, wanting to understand Israel first-hand before arguing about it, and wanting to give back.
- **Security is still a live question for families.** Programs relocate students when needed and point to Masa's safety infrastructure. Conflicts with Lebanon and Iran did not reduce 2026 applications. The finder should still show each program's safety and evacuation policy.

## 2. The landscape: seven types of program

| # | Category | What it is | Examples |
|---|----------|------------|----------|
| 1 | **Pluralistic experiential** | Apartments in Jerusalem and/or Tel Aviv, internships or volunteering, ulpan, trips. Little religious structure. | Young Judaea Year Course, Aardvark, Kivunim |
| 2 | **Pluralistic Jewish learning** | Serious text study for a mixed-denomination group, often with Israeli peers | Hevruta (Hartman), Kol Ami Mechina |
| 3 | **Denominational movement** | Run by or with a movement or its youth group | Nativ (Conservative), Shnat Netzer (Reform), Bnei Akiva Kadima (Modern Orthodox) |
| 4 | **Zionist youth movement "Shnat"** | Leadership year that feeds staff back into the movement's summer camps | Habonim Dror Workshop, Hashomer Hatzair Shnat, Maccabi |
| 5 | **University-based** | Credit-bearing first year on an Israeli campus | TAU ELI, Hebrew University Rothberg First-Year, Bar-Ilan Israel XP |
| 6 | **Mechina (pre-army)** | Live and study with Israeli 18-year-olds before they enlist. Physical training, leadership, Hebrew immersion. | Kol Ami, Bnei Akiva Mechina Olamit, Yachad (Joint Council of Mechinot) |
| 7 | **Yeshiva / Midrasha / Seminary** | Full-time Torah study, single-gender, Orthodox | Har Etzion, Lev HaTorah, Orayta, Migdal HaTorah (men); Midreshet Lindenbaum, Migdal Oz, Nishmat, MMY (women) |
| + | **Army pathway** | Not a gap year: aliyah and IDF service, or a short taste of the army | Garin Tzabar (aliyah + IDF); Marva (2-month IDF experience, an add-on on Aardvark and Year Course) |

The independent directory **yearinisrael.com** lists 132 programs: 65 yeshivas, 46 seminaries and 21 gap year programs. Masa lists 36 yeshivas alone. For a school audience the important half of the market is categories 1–6, because existing guides mostly cover category 7.

**Price picture:** The most popular non-Orthodox programs cost **$25k–$40k** for the full year. Masa's own comparison table spans **$13k–$38k**. Mechinot are the outlier at **$10k–$13k**. Yeshivas and seminaries were not priced in this pass and need checking.

## 4. Money: what families need to know

- **The Masa grant** is the base subsidy for almost every program here. Most programs subtract it automatically. Amounts vary by program length and need; sources disagree on figures (NCSY lists $1,000–$3,700, another guide says up to $4,500). **Confirm the 2027–28 numbers with Masa directly.**
- **Need-based aid** comes from Masa, from the programs themselves (Kivunim asks for a copy of the FAFSA), and from **local Federations**. Atlanta's Federation gives up to $10,000. Boston's CJP should be checked.
- **529 plans generally cannot be used** for non-accredited gap programs. Credit-bearing university programs may be an exception; check each one.
- **Hidden costs:** flights, meals (Aardvark), a monthly allowance, optional trips abroad, and transcript fees (Kivunim $1,700, JTS $1,000/semester).

### What application details are public

- **Hevruta** is the only program whose full application form is public. Its six written prompts and the 60-second video prompt are in the app word for word. It also needs one reference (not family), a photo, and a $100 non-refundable fee, and you are interviewed only after the form is complete.
- **Year Course:** Regpack portal, $100 non-refundable fee, $5,000 deposit. Payments are due Jan 1, Mar 1, Jun 1 and Aug 1, 2027. Cancel before Jun 1 and you lose only the $100.
- **Bnei Akiva Kadima:** 2027–28 is ₪118,000. You apply, then interview, then hear shortly afterward. ₪15,000 deposit.
- **TAU ELI:** $14,000 per semester or $28,000 per year including housing. GPA 3.0+. The A/2 visa must be in hand before arrival.
- **Kivunim:** essays of 500+ words; prompts not public. Aid requires a copy of your FAFSA plus a written request.
- All the other programs keep their essay prompts, recommendation requirements and interview format inside the portal. The app marks these "Not published" and lets students paste prompts in themselves.

### Visa (A/2) basics

- Masa programs: apply at the consulate before leaving (recommended), or do an in-Israel change of status through the program. You need a photo, a birth-certificate copy and the Masa participation letter.
- University programs (TAU, HUJI): the visa is required before arrival. Apply about two months ahead; the passport usually needs long validity (TAU says 18 months).
- **Children of Israeli citizens** are generally Israeli citizens themselves. They cannot get the student visa, must enter on an Israeli passport, and must settle military status with the consulate (from age 16 and 4 months).

## 6. Questions the finder should ask

These are the questions that separate the programs most sharply:

1. **Jewish practice and community:** pluralistic / egalitarian / Modern Orthodox / Orthodox / secular or cultural. Includes Shabbat and kashrut expectations.
2. **How much Jewish text study:** none / some / substantial / full-time.
3. **Main focus:** internships and career, volunteering and service, academics and credit, leadership, army prep, travel, outdoors, sports, tech, arts and culinary.
4. **Israeli peers:** do they want to live with Israelis (mechina / Hevruta), or are they fine with an international group?
5. **Independence:** cooking for themselves in an apartment, or a meal plan and structured days?
6. **City:** Jerusalem, Tel Aviv, both, kibbutz or periphery.
7. **Length:** semester or full year.
8. **College credit:** is it needed? (Some colleges treat credit as transfer status; students should check with the college they are deferring.)
9. **Travel beyond Israel:** none / optional / central (Kivunim).
10. **Budget band:** under $15k, $15–25k, $25–35k, $35k+.
11. **Gender setting:** co-ed or single-gender.
12. **Hebrew level:** some mechinot move into Hebrew-only mid-year.
